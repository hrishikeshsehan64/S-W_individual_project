Explore the wonders of civilizations that shaped our world

Historical Timeline

3500 BCE

Birth of Writing

Sumerians in Mesopotamia develop cuneiform, one of the earliest known writing systems, revolutionizing communication and record-keeping.

2560 BCE

Great Pyramid Built

The Great Pyramid of Giza is completed under Pharaoh Khufu, standing as a testament to Egyptian engineering and remaining the tallest structure for 3,800 years.

1754 BCE

Code of Hammurabi

King Hammurabi of Babylon establishes one of the earliest and most complete written legal codes, containing 282 laws governing ancient Babylonian society.

776 BCE

First Olympic Games

The ancient Greeks hold the first recorded Olympic Games in Olympia, establishing a tradition of athletic competition that continues today.

221 BCE

Great Wall Construction

Emperor Qin Shi Huang orders the connection of various defensive walls, creating the foundation of the Great Wall of China.

27 BCE

Roman Empire Begins

Augustus becomes the first Roman Emperor, marking the transition from Roman Republic to Roman Empire and beginning the Pax Romana.

Great Ancient Civilizations

Ancient Egypt

3100 BCE - 30 BCE

One of the world's greatest civilizations, known for pyramids, hieroglyphics, advanced mathematics, and a complex religious system centered around the afterlife.

Ancient Greece

800 BCE - 146 BCE

The birthplace of democracy, philosophy, and Western civilization. Home to great thinkers like Socrates, Plato, and Aristotle who shaped modern thought.

Roman Empire

27 BCE - 476 CE

A vast empire that unified much of Europe and the Mediterranean, leaving lasting legacies in law, engineering, language, and governance.

Mesopotamia

3500 BCE - 539 BCE

Known as the "Cradle of Civilization," this region saw the birth of writing, the wheel, and the first cities. Home to Sumerians, Babylonians, and Assyrians.

Ancient China

1600 BCE - 220 CE

An advanced civilization that gave the world paper, gunpowder, the compass, and printing. Known for the Great Wall, Terracotta Army, and philosophical traditions.

Indus Valley

3300 BCE - 1300 BCE

One of the world's earliest urban civilizations with advanced city planning, drainage systems, and standardized weights and measures.

Ancient India - Cradle of Wisdom

Indus Valley Civilization

3300 BCE - 1300 BCE

One of the world's oldest urban civilizations with sophisticated cities like Mohenjo-daro and Harappa. Featured advanced drainage systems, standardized bricks, and mysterious undeciphered script.

Vedic Period

1500 BCE - 500 BCE

Era of the Vedas, ancient Sanskrit texts that form the foundation of Hindu philosophy. Established complex rituals, social structures, and early mathematical concepts including zero.

Maurya Empire

321 BCE - 185 BCE

India's first major empire under Chandragupta Maurya and later Ashoka the Great. Ashoka's edicts promoting Buddhism, non-violence, and welfare are inscribed on pillars across India.

Gupta Empire (Golden Age)

320 CE - 550 CE

Known as India's Golden Age. Aryabhata calculated pi, Kalidasa wrote Sanskrit masterpieces, and advancements in mathematics, astronomy, medicine, and art flourished.

Buddhist Era

6th Century BCE onwards

Buddha's teachings spread from India across Asia. Universities like Nalanda and Takshashila became centers of learning, attracting scholars from across the ancient world.

Scientific Achievements

Ancient Era

Ancient Indians invented the decimal system, concept of zero, Ayurveda medicine, yoga, chess, and made groundbreaking discoveries in metallurgy, including rust-resistant iron pillars.

2600 BCE

Harappa & Mohenjo-daro

Advanced cities with grid planning, covered drainage, public baths, and multi-story buildings demonstrate sophisticated urban planning centuries ahead of their time.

1500 BCE

Rigveda Composed

The oldest of the four Vedas is composed, containing over 1,000 hymns. It remains one of the world's oldest religious texts still in use today.

563 BCE

Birth of Buddha

Siddhartha Gautama is born in Lumbini. He later attains enlightenment and teaches the Middle Path, founding Buddhism which spreads across Asia.

326 BCE

Alexander Reaches India

Alexander the Great's campaign reaches India but is stopped at the Hydaspes River after facing King Porus, marking the eastern limit of Greek expansion.

268 BCE

Ashoka's Reign

Emperor Ashoka embraces Buddhism after the bloody Kalinga War, spreading messages of peace, tolerance, and dharma throughout his vast empire.

400 CE

Nalanda University

Nalanda becomes the world's first residential university with thousands of students and teachers from across Asia studying mathematics, astronomy, medicine, and philosophy.

Fascinating Facts

📜

Ancient Library

The Library of Alexandria once held over 700,000 scrolls, making it the largest library of the ancient world.

🏛️

Concrete Innovation

Roman concrete was so advanced that some structures like the Pantheon still stand today, 2,000 years later.

⚔️

Alexander's Empire

Alexander the Great conquered most of the known world by age 30, creating an empire stretching from Greece to India.

🎭

Greek Theater

Ancient Greek theaters could seat up to 14,000 people and had such perfect acoustics that whispers on stage could be heard in the back row.
